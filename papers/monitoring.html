Accounting-Based Governance in Supplier–Customer Relationships

Xuanheng Huang (solo-authored)

Abstract

I investigate the role of accounting in material supply agreements filed with the SEC from 2000 to 2023. Using a large language model (LLM), I identify provisions governing the production, dissemination, and verification of accounting information within the supplier–customer relationship. I find that 60.5% of agreements contain at least one such provision and that customers monitor suppliers more often than in the opposite direction. Supplier-targeted monitoring is more likely under cost-plus pricing, whereas customer-targeted monitoring is more likely when customers are financially weaker. Bidirectional monitoring is more common when the parties subject to oversight have stronger financial reporting quality. After contract initiation, monitored suppliers exhibit higher subsequent sales, while customers holding information and verification rights over suppliers exhibit both higher sales and lower operating-cost ratios. These effects are concentrated in agreements in which the monitored party has lower pre-signing financial reporting quality. Customers' operating improvements are stronger under cost-plus pricing, while suppliers' improvements are stronger under fixed-price contracts. Overall, the findings suggest that the direction of accounting information flows reflects distinct economic incentives and predicts subsequent firm performance.
