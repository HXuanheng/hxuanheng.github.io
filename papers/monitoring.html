Accounting-Based Governance in Supplier–Customer Relationships

Xuanheng Huang (solo-authored)

Abstract

I study provisions that regulate the exchange of private accounting information in 2,043 supply contracts filed with the SEC. Using a large language model (LLM), I find that these provisions appear in more than two-thirds of the contracts and are more frequently used to monitor suppliers than customers. Customers are more likely to monitor suppliers with higher financial reporting quality and when contracts use cost-plus pricing. After contract initiation, customers with monitoring rights exhibit lower operating costs relative to sales, particularly under cost-plus pricing. Suppliers are more likely to monitor customers that are financially weaker and when contracts include royalty-based compensation. Suppliers with monitoring rights hold less cash after contract initiation. Quasi-experimental evidence shows that suppliers affected by SOX Section 404 are more likely to accept record-keeping obligations, and customers exposed to the 2018–2019 Section 301 tariffs are more likely to be monitored by their suppliers. Overall, the findings suggest that the direction of accounting information flows reflects distinct economic incentives on the two sides of the supply relationship.
