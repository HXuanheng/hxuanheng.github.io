## Teaching experience and philosophy

In 2026 I am the lecturer for *Financial Statement Analysis for Company Valuation* at Bocconi (97 enrolled students, master's level), where I have designed the course around an explicit integration of canonical FSA frameworks (Penman and Pope) with hands-on AI tools that students can deploy in equity research workflows. Earlier, as a Teaching Assistant since 2023, I supported three courses spanning the FSA-and-valuation curriculum: *Financial Statement Analysis for Value Investing* (where I also contributed materials to the textbook authored by Stephen Penman and Peter Pope), *Accounting and Financial Statement Analysis* (Prof. Antonio Marra), and *Accounting for Value* (Prof. Lauren Vollon). Across these settings I have run case discussions, designed problem sets, supervised valuation projects, and held office hours for student cohorts of mixed quantitative background.

My philosophy is simple: students learn valuation by *doing* it, not by being told about it. I structure each session around a real firm, walk students through reformulating its financial statements, ask them to make and defend a forecast, and then confront their forecast with what the market priced in. The introduction of AI in the 2026 course has sharpened this approach — students now treat the AI as a fast (but fallible) research assistant whose output they must interrogate, which is exactly the disposition a recruiter at PwC, Goldman, or a buy-side fund would want. I am especially keen to bring this approach into Leeds's BSc Accounting and Finance, MSc Accounting and Finance, and MSc Banking and International Finance programmes, and into any Executive Education work where AI-augmented financial analysis is increasingly demanded.

## Practice-oriented scholarship and pedagogic interests

My research and my pedagogic interests are tightly linked. My solo-authored job market paper, *Accounting-Based Monitoring: Evidence from Supply Chain Contracts*, uses material supply agreements filed with the SEC (2000–2023) to show that 31% of contracts embed accounting-based monitoring clauses (most commonly customer-held verification rights), and that intensive monitoring measurably improves operating efficiency, reduces cost volatility, and shortens cash-conversion cycles. The paper sits at the intersection of managerial accounting, contract design, and auditing — a natural fit for case material in accounting modules and for practitioner-facing scholarship outputs (Accountancy, professional bodies, executive briefings). My coauthored paper *Are Disclosed Merger Synergies Realized?* (with Ellahie, Tuna, and Vincenzi) examines over 12,000 U.S. M&A deals and is being prepared for journal submission; alongside its empirical contribution, it generates rich teaching material on disclosure credibility, post-merger integration, and goodwill impairment — content that will sit directly inside an MSc Accounting and Finance corporate-reporting module. A third paper with Peter Pope on operating-versus-financial leverage in asset pricing develops factor measures from accounting fundamentals and provides a concrete pedagogic bridge from accounting variables to capital markets — natural material for any MSc Banking and International Finance asset-pricing block.
