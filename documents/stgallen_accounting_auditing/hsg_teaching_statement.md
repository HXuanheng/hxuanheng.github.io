# Teaching Statement

**Xuanheng Huang** | PhD Candidate, Bocconi University | xuanheng.huang@phd.unibocconi.it

---

**Philosophy.** I teach valuation and financial statement analysis the way it is actually practised — as the disciplined, case-by-case translation of accounting numbers into economic claims about a business. Lecture material is built around real firms, real filings, and real questions an investor, lender, or acquirer must answer with the data on hand. Each session asks students to (i) reformulate the financial statements, (ii) make a defensible forecast, (iii) translate that forecast into a value, and (iv) confront their value with the price the market is quoting. The discipline lies in showing how each step is sensitive to specific accounting choices and disclosure quality — which is exactly the connective tissue between FSA, auditing, and management accounting that HSG students will need.

**Evidence so far.** In 2026 I am the lecturer for *Financial Statement Analysis for Company Valuation* at Bocconi University (97 enrolled master's students), where I integrated AI-augmented research workflows into the syllabus. Since 2023 I have served as Teaching Assistant for three Bocconi courses spanning the FSA-and-valuation curriculum: *Financial Statement Analysis for Value Investing* (I also contributed materials to the Penman–Pope textbook), *Accounting and Financial Statement Analysis* (Prof. Marra), and *Accounting for Value* (Prof. Vollon). Across these settings I have run case discussions, designed problem sets, and held weekly office hours for students of mixed quantitative background. Numeric evaluations are enclosed in the separate teaching-evaluations document.

**Integrating AI into the classroom.** In the 2026 Bocconi course I introduced AI as a fast — but fallible — research assistant whose output students must critically interrogate. Three workflows: (i) using LLMs to pull material from 10-K filings and conference calls, then independently auditing the extraction; (ii) prompting an LLM to draft a valuation thesis, then identifying its specific accounting mistakes; (iii) using AI to stress-test forecasts under alternative assumptions. The pedagogic goal is to graduate students who can use AI productively *and* know exactly where it breaks — precisely what HSG executive-education audiences ask for.

Natural fits with my background include: financial statement analysis, equity valuation, corporate reporting, management accounting, auditing and internal control over inter-organisational relationships, and AI in financial analysis.
