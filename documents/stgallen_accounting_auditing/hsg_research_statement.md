# Research Statement

**Xuanheng Huang** | PhD Candidate, Bocconi University | xuanheng.huang@phd.unibocconi.it

---

My research programme studies how accounting information supports valuation across a firm's stakeholders — equity investors, debtholders, M&A participants, and supply-chain partners. The unifying premise is that accounting fundamentals carry valuation-relevant signals that markets and contracting parties price imperfectly, and that disclosure, contract design, and AI-augmented information processing shape those signals in measurable ways. I work primarily with empirical-archival methods, hand-collected contract data, and large-scale text analysis.

My **solo-authored job market paper**, *Accounting-Based Monitoring: Evidence from Supply Chain Contracts*, opens up an under-studied locus of accounting use — inter-firm contracts. Using SEC-filed material supply agreements between 2000 and 2023, I document that 31% of contracts embed accounting-based monitoring clauses; in over 70% of these cases the customer holds verification rights over the supplier's accounting information. Customers impose more intensive monitoring when suppliers face higher cost volatility or cost-adjusted pricing; suppliers monitor more when customers are financially weaker. Intensive customer monitoring is associated with improved operating efficiency, reduced cost volatility, and shorter cash-conversion cycles for both parties. The paper recasts accounting as a contracting technology and speaks directly to literatures on auditing, internal control, and management accounting — a natural fit with HSG's Management Accounting and Financial Leadership priorities.

**Two further papers** extend the agenda. *Are Disclosed Merger Synergies Realized?* (with Atif Ellahie, Irem Tuna, and Roberto Vincenzi), in final revisions before journal submission, examines synergy disclosures in over 12,000 U.S. M&A transactions (2004–2021) using press releases and conference-call transcripts. Disclosing firms earn higher announcement returns, but disclosed synergies are systematically over-optimistic — more intensive disclosures predict lower post-merger operating performance and more frequent goodwill impairments. *Leverage and Operating Risk in Asset Pricing: An Accounting Perspective* (with Peter Pope) revisits the Fama–French six-factor model by recomputing factors using operating characteristics while separately accounting for financial leverage. The resulting model subsumes FF6 and outperforms the q-factor model, with return on net operating assets and growth in net operating assets driving the improvement.

**Pipeline.** A new project with Peter Pope, *Characteristic-Based Factors and Exposure to Macroeconomic Risks*, asks whether accounting-based factor returns reflect compensation for macroeconomic-risk exposure. Looking ahead, I plan to deepen the supply-chain agenda along three margins: (i) the audit and internal-control implications of customer verification rights — central to HSG's audit-and-assurance portfolio; (ii) the role of AI in inter-firm disclosure and contract monitoring; and (iii) digitalisation as a moderator of supply-chain contract design. My target outlets are the FT-50 accounting journals (TAR, JAR, JAE, RAST, CAR) and the leading finance journals.

I see strong complementarities with the HSG Accounting and Auditing group's lines on management accounting, financial leadership, digitalisation, and audit/assurance, and I would aim to build joint projects with HSG colleagues from day one.
